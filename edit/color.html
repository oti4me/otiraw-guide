OtiRAW User Guide

Color

OtiRAW has several colour tools, from simple to precise. You rarely need more than one or two on a photo. This table says which to reach for.

You want to…Use
Make one colour more or less saturated, lighter, darker, or shift its hueColor Mixer
Change one exact colour you can point at (a dress, a wall)Point Color
Tint the shadows and highlights for a moodColor Grading
Balance and grade the way a colourist does, with wheelsPrimaries
Push colours around freely on a map of hue and saturationColor Warper
Shift a hue’s saturation and density the way film doesColor Slice
Change one property against another along a curveColor Curves
Convert to black and white, or mix channelsChannel Mixer
Correct the camera’s primariesCalibration

All of them can also be used inside a mask, on part of the photo.

Color Mixer

Eight colour bands (red, orange, yellow, green, aqua, blue, purple, magenta), each with Hue, Saturation and Luminance.

The Adjust buttons choose how they are laid out: all three properties for every band, one property across all bands, or one band’s three sliders together.

Typical uses: darken a blue sky with blue Luminance; calm an over-bright green with green Saturation; warm skin by nudging orange Hue.

Point Color

Point Color (the second tab of the Color Mixer) adjusts one colour you pick, together with the colours like it.

  1. Click the Point Color Dropper and click the colour on the photo. (Esc stops picking.) A swatch is added.
  2. In the colour field, drag across for Hue Shift and up and down for Sat. Shift; drag the bar beside it for Lum. Shift. Hold ⌥ for finer moves.
  3. Set how wide the selection is with Range, or open Show Ranges for separate Hue Range, Saturation Range and Luminance Range.
  4. Tick Visualize Range to see the photo grey wherever the swatch selects nothing.

Each swatch’s menu has Reset Swatch, Delete Swatch, Delete All Swatches, and Make Mask from Swatch, which turns the selection into a mask you can apply any adjustment to.

Colours too close to grey or black can’t be picked: Point Color selects by hue.

Color Grading

Four colour wheels: Shadows, Midtones, Highlights and Global. Dragging in a wheel tints that range of tones towards the colour you drag to; the further out, the stronger.

ControlWhat it does
The wheelHue and saturation of the tint. ⇧ for finer moves; double-click to clear
The slider under a wheelThat range’s Luminance
BlendingHow much the ranges overlap
BalanceWhether more of the photo counts as shadows or as highlights

The classic use is a cool tint in the shadows with a warm one in the highlights.

Primaries

Colourist’s controls, as used for film and video.

HDR Zones adjust exposure, saturation and tint by tone, in zones from black to specular highlights (Dark, Shad, Light, High, Spec and a Global Zone). Click a zone to change it; drag a boundary to move where it starts; Falloff softens the edge between zones.

Log Wheels (Shadow, Midtone, Highlight and Offset, with Low Range and High Range) each move only their own tones.

Color Warper

A grid laid over the colours: hue round the circle, saturation outwards. Drag a point and the colours there move, taking their neighbours smoothly with them.

ToDo this
Find a colour on the gridClick the Color Warper Dropper, then the colour on the photo
Move a colourDrag its point
Select several points⇧-click them
Hold a colour still while others moveRight-click its point to pin it
Put a point backDouble-click it

Grid sets how many points there are (a new size keeps your moves). Falloff sets how far a move spreads. The Chroma-Luma tab does the same on a map of saturation against brightness: drag up to lighten a colour, down to darken it.

Color Slice

Six hue bands and skin, each with:

ControlWhat it does
DensityDarker and richer, or lighter and thinner, the way dye does on film
SaturationThe band’s saturation
Density Depth, Sat. DepthHow much the deeper, more saturated colours are affected
Center, WidthWhich hues the band covers

Click the hue strip to choose a band; drag a band’s handle to move its centre. A view button shows the photo grey wherever the chosen band reaches nothing.

Color Curves

Click Advanced in the Color Mixer to open Color Curves in its own window. Each curve changes one property along another:

CurveChanges
Hue vs HueA hue into another hue
Hue vs SatA hue’s saturation
Hue vs LumA hue’s brightness
Lum vs SatSaturation by brightness (desaturate the shadows, for instance)
Sat vs SatSaturation by saturation (boost only the muted colours)
Sat vs LumBrightness by saturation
  • Click the curve to add a point with an anchor either side (hold ⌥ for the point alone), and drag it. ⇧-drag moves it up and down only.
  • Right-click, double-click or press Delete to remove a point.
  • The Curves Dropper puts a point at a colour you click on the photo.
  • The buttons under the curve add points at common places: Reds, Skin Tones, Yellows, Greens, Cyans, Blues, Magentas; or Shadows, Midtones, Highlights.
  • Reset Curve flattens the one shown; Reset All flattens all of them.

Channel Mixer

  • Color: each output channel (red, green, blue) is a mix of the three input channels.
  • Black & White: one mix of red, green and blue makes the grey. More red lightens skin and darkens skies, as a red filter does on black-and-white film.

Total shows what the three add up to; 100 % keeps the brightness.

Calibration

Adjusts how the camera’s own primaries are read: Shadows Tint, and Hue and Saturation for the red, green and blue primaries. Small moves here change the whole character of a photo’s colour, which is why many presets use it.

Black and white

Three ways, from quickest to most controlled:

  1. Channel Mixer › Black & White: set the mix.
  2. The Looks tool’s Black & White with a colour filter, with or without a film stock.
  3. A black-and-white film stock in the Looks tool, with its own grain and tone.
