OtiRAW User Guide

Looks, film and negatives

The Looks tool (⇧L) gives a photo a character in one step: a ready-made look, a LUT, a film stock, or the conversion of a photographed negative.

A look sits under your edit. Essentials, colour and masks all still work on top of it.

Profile

The Profile menu at the top chooses how the camera’s colours are read:

ProfileWhat it is
OtiRAW StandardThe camera’s colour data, OtiRAW’s default
EmbeddedThe profile a DNG carries, with its colour tables
Your own.dcp camera profiles you added with Add Profiles…

Remove moves an added profile out of the library, to the Trash. A photo whose profile is missing renders with the standard one and says so.

Looks

Looks are in tabs:

TabHolds
The built-in groupsCamera and film looks that come with OtiRAW
MatchedLooks you saved from Match Look, matched afresh on each photo
CustomYour own LUTs, imported as .cube files

Click a look to apply it. Amount fades it in and out. None removes the look or LUT and turns film emulation off.

Your own LUTs

  • Add LUTs… imports .cube files (choose several with ⇧ or ⌘).
  • Add Folder… imports every .cube file in a folder and its subfolders.
  • Type in the filter field to find one by name or folder.
  • Remove moves the chosen LUT out of the library, to the Trash.

LUTs are kept in OtiRAW’s own library, so a photo keeps its look if the original file moves. If a photo’s LUT isn’t in the library (it was removed, or the photo was edited on another computer), the photo renders without it and says so.

Adjusting a look

Under the looks, Tone and Colour adjust the look itself:

ControlWhat it does
ContrastThe look’s contrast
ShoulderHow gently highlights roll off
FadeLifts the blacks for a matte look
Saturation, Warmth, TintThe look’s colour
CrossoverOpposite tints in shadows and highlights, as cross-processed film

Reset to Look puts the film emulation back as the look sets it.

Film Emulation

Film emulation renders the photo the way film would: each dye layer’s response, the layers’ effect on one another, then the print.

SectionWhat it does
Film StockThe Stock to use, with Exposure (how the film was exposed), Development (pushed or pulled) and Interlayer (how much the dye layers affect each other)
Colour HeadThe enlarger’s filtration: Cyan–Red, Magenta–Green, Yellow–Blue
ExpandSets the Black and White points of the scan
GrainAmount, Size and Roughness. A film format sizes the grain to the frame, as a smaller film is enlarged more; Pixels keeps it the same size in any photo
Halation & BloomHalation (the red glow round bright lights) with its Radius, an option for film without an anti-halation backing (a wider, redder glow), and Bloom
PrintThe paper the negative is printed on. A slide is seen as it is, not printed, so for slide stocks these are its scan’s settings
Vignette, Leaks & DustVignette, Light Leaks and Dust & Scratches. Shuffle Leaks and Shuffle Dust give another arrangement
FrameA film border drawn round the photo, which grows to hold it. It shows while the Looks tool is open, and in exports
Black & WhiteA black-and-white rendering with a colour filter: the filter lightens its own colour and darkens its opposite, as on a lens

Negative Conversion

For a negative or slide you photographed with your camera (on a light box, with a macro lens).

  1. Choose the kind:
KindWhat it does
OffNot film: no conversion
ColourA colour negative (C-41): inverted, with the orange base taken out
B&WA black-and-white negative: inverted and made neutral
SlideSlide film: not inverted; its base and range are made right
  1. Set the Film Base: click Pick and click the unexposed edge of the film on the photo. Or leave it on Auto to have it found.
  2. Click Analyse. OtiRAW finds the picture inside the film’s edge, the holder and the light box, and measures each channel’s range.
  3. Crop to the picture for the closest result.
ControlWhat it does
BorderHow much of the frame’s edge the analysis ignores
DensityThe print’s overall density
Auto BalanceThe conversion sets the white balance; Essentials’ is set aside while it is on
ChannelAll, or Red, Green or Blue alone: one channel can be cleaner on a stained or tinted base
Colour BalanceEach channel’s Shadows and Highlights against the others’, for cross-processed or expired film
Light Box › FalloffCorrects a light box that is brighter in the middle

Messages tell you when something is off: too little of the frame to analyse (try a smaller border), or a film base that is clipped in the scan (photograph the negative a little darker).

Match Look

Match Look copies the look of a reference picture onto your photos. It is described with presets, in Presets, copy, sync and Match Look.

Export a look as a LUT

Photo › Export LUT… (⌥⇧⌘L) saves the active photo’s colour and tone as a .cube 3D LUT for use in video and other software.

SettingChoices
InputThe encoding the LUT expects: Rec. 709, sRGB or one of eight camera log formats
OutputRec. 709, sRGB or Rec. 2020
Size17, 33 or 65 points a side

The LUT holds the edit’s global colour and tone: white balance, exposure, contrast, whites, blacks, dehaze, the colour tools, the tone curve, calibration, the channel mixer and the Looks tool’s film colour, tone and LUT. It can’t hold anything local (masks, sharpening, grain, geometry).

A preset can be exported the same way: right-click it and choose Export as LUT….
