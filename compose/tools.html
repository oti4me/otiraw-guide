OtiRAW User Guide

Tools

The tools strip runs down the left of the Compose workspace. Some buttons hold a group of related tools: hover over (or press and hold) the button to see the rest.

The bar over the canvas always shows the settings of the tool in hand. Right-click the strip or the bar to set how large their icons are.

Foreground and background colours

The two squares at the foot of the strip:

  • The foreground colour is what new shapes, text and brush strokes are drawn in. A layer keeps the colour it was made with: changing the foreground later doesn’t recolour it.
  • The background colour is what a click with the Rectangle tool fills the canvas with, and what borders and added canvas are filled with.

X swaps them. Default Colors puts back black over white. Click a square to choose a colour; the Studio’s Color and Swatches tabs choose colours too, with harmonies (complementary, analogous, triadic…) and your recent colours.

The Studio’s Sliders tab sets the colour with six sliders: Hue, Saturation and Brightness, then Red, Green and Blue. Each slider’s bar shows the colours it runs through with the others left as they are, so you can see where to drag. The hex code is beside the two colour squares; click a square to choose which colour the sliders set.

Reference photos

The References panel sits at the bottom left of the canvas. It holds photos to look at while you work and to take colours from. Click its name to open or fold it.

ToDo this
Add photosClick Add… and choose one or several. Photos, JPEGs, PNGs and TIFFs all work
Take a colourClick the photo: the colour under the pointer becomes the foreground colour. Drag to keep sampling
Look closerDrag the zoom slider, pinch, or hold ⌘ and scroll. Fit shows all of it again
Move about when zoomedScroll
Show anotherClick it in the strip
Take one off the listRemove. The file itself is not touched
Hide the panelView › Canvas › Show References, or right-click the canvas

You don’t need the Eyedropper for this: clicking a reference takes its colour with any tool in hand, so with the Brush you can click a reference and paint straight away. The list of references is remembered between sessions and is the same for every composition.

Brush sizes are in pixels of the canvas on every tool that paints (Brush, Remove and the retouching tools, the Toning tools and Liquify). A composition made from a photo has no size of its own until it is first saved; until then the sizes are counted in the photo’s own pixels, which is close but can differ if the photo is cropped. The status bar says “About … pixels, its photo’s” while that is so.

Over the canvas the brush’s cursor has the shape of its tip, at the size, angle and roundness it will paint with:

TipCursor
Round (Soft Round, Hard Round, Airbrush, Ink Pen, Pencil, Watercolor)A ring
Square (Marker, Confetti)A square, or a bar when it is squashed
Chalk (Chalk, Charcoal)A rough-edged disc; rougher the softer the brush
SpatterIts drops
Bristle (Dry Brush)Its bristles’ lines

Chalk, spatter and bristle tips fall a little differently at each dab, so their cursor shows one such dab. A brush too small on screen to show a shape is a ring.

The Brushes section

The Brushes section, on the right, lists every brush with a stroke painted by it. Click its name to open it.

  • Click a brush to paint with it: it takes up the Brush tool too.
  • A brush whose tip is a picture (a stamp) is shown as that picture, not as a stroke.
  • Brushes are in groups: Basic (the ready brushes), My Brushes (those you saved), and a group for each brush file you imported, named after the file. Click a group’s name to fold it.
  • Type in Search brushes to show only the brushes with those letters in their names.
  • Each brush’s size is at the right of its row, in the canvas’s pixels.
  • Import…, Save Brush and Delete Brush are at the foot. Delete Brush removes the brush in hand if it is one you saved or imported; the ready brushes can’t be deleted.
  • Right-clicking one of your brushes also offers Delete Brush. Right-click an imported group’s name for Delete Group, which removes all its brushes; Edit › Delete Brush Group does the same for the group of the brush in hand.

Importing brushes

Import Brushes… (in the Brushes section, the Brush menu and the Edit menu) reads Photoshop brush files (.abr). Choose one file or several; their brushes are added after your saved ones, under the names they have in the file, in a group named after the file.

What comes across:

From the fileResult
The tip: a round tip’s size, hardness, angle and roundness, or a picture (a stamp)Kept. A picture larger than 256 pixels is made smaller
Spacing, Shape Dynamics, Scattering, Color Dynamics, TransferKept
Opacity, flow, blend mode, smoothing, wet edges, build-upKept
TextureThe depth is kept; the app’s own grain stands in for the file’s pattern
A dual brushIts second tip is kept, not its own spacing and scatter
Bristle tipsThe app’s bristle tip stands in
Erodible and airbrush tips, Noise, flip jitter, Purity, Brush Pose, Stylus Wheel and Rotation controlsNot kept

If anything wasn’t kept, or a file couldn’t be read, a window says so when the import is done. An imported brush looks like its original but won’t paint exactly as it did there.

A brush file gives each brush’s width in pixels. The brush is made that wide on the canvas you have open, and keeps that share of the canvas on others. Delete an imported brush with Delete Brush, like one of your own, or the whole file’s brushes with Delete Group.

Remove and retouching

Remove (J) has three modes:

ModeWhat it does
RemoveFills what you paint over with what plausibly lies behind it
Heal⌥-click where to take from, then paint: its detail goes in, in the place’s own colour and tone
Clone⌥-click where to take from, then paint: it is copied as it is

Fixed Source keeps the source on the spot you chose while you paint; off, it follows the brush at a constant distance. Try Another fills a removal another way.

Retouching is kept on a retouch layer, so it can be changed or deleted later.

Liquify

Drag over the chosen layer to reshape it: Forward Warp pushes, Pucker pinches, Bloat swells, Twirl turns, and Reconstruct paints it back. Size and Pressure set the brush. Restore All undoes every Liquify stroke on the layer.

Actions

An action records steps to play back on other compositions.

  1. Open the Studio’s Actions tab and click Record Action.
  2. Do the steps: adjustment layers, filters, effects, turns and flips.
  3. Click Stop Recording.

Play runs it on the open composition. Batch… runs it on every photo selected in the grid and exports each one. See Save and export a composition.
